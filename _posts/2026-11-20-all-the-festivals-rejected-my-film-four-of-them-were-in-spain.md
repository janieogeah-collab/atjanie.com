My last film, a 14-minute dramedy, went into twelve festivals. It received one selection, ten rejections, and one is still under consideration. 

For someone self-teaching and still pretty green behind the ears, I’ve mostly been fumbling my way around, watching and learning from the experienced but, more importantly, learning from my mistakes. Yes, mistakes are exactly what I’d call my festival run so far. 

Before we get into that, the said film was submitted to the following festivals:



April Fools Short Film Challenge

Tehran International Short Film Festival

The 9th Jeonju International Short Film Festival

Festival Internacional de Cortometrajes del Geoparque de Granada - Cortos En Cortes

Festival Internacional De Cine De Montanejos

Cortesina Festival de Cortometrajes

Cinemana Mare Nostrum

Zero To Hero Short Film Festival

African Diaspora Cinema Festival

AFRIFF

Barcelona Indie Filmmakers Festival • BARCIFF



Plus the one still under consideration that I’d be leaving out of the list for now.

This list includes both paid and free submissions; albeit ‘free’ on Festhome, a 3.19 Euro platform fee is still required per submission, so it wasn’t exactly free.

Why ten rejections?

At first, I blamed the film, believing it wasn’t good enough. A part of me still believes that, perhaps, the part that is yet to make something exceptionally beautiful that, every time I see it, I get orgasmic pleasure. 

But seriously, I may not know the whole shebang of how festivals work; I have, however, gained a decent amount of understanding that a rejection is the least informative signal there is about a film’s quality. 

This understanding also comes from having three cold viewers (people who don’t know me) assess my film, along with a list of the festivals that said no. The following are the realisations drawn from their feedback:

**A hard sell to Spanish festivals.**

Four of the festivals were organised in Spain for the Spanish audience, and because these small and mid-sized festivals get flooded through Festhome and FilmFreeway, preference is often given to works made locally. For its fifth edition, the Cortisena Fest received 768 works from 47 countries, with 35 short films sent in from the province of Alicante, the home of the festival. Makes you wonder why the rules page says ‘open to international submissions’ if local works were the priority. Perhaps for programmers who collect submission fees, what they simply mean to say on those pages is ‘your submission fee is very much welcome’. Now whether your film is given a fair shot is another matter. 

**A yes from Tehran was close to impossible.** 

So this story implied the use of cocaine, ‘fuck’ was said out loud, a pastizzi nailed to a cross with men praying to it, plus a naked man insert (though concealed) in the post-credit scene. Under Iranian content rules, these were already four reasons to pass on the film even before evaluating its narrative quality or technical flaws.

**Jeonju was a very long shot.**

While comically referenced terms like the pastizzi, LovinMalta, ODZ (a Maltese planning term) and some Igbo and Maltese slang may have earned understanding from the Maltese audience and perhaps the Igbos of Nigeria, they were lost on the Korean programmers despite subtitles.

**The rejections worth chewing on.**

The African Diaspora Cinema and AFRIFF appeared to be the most fitting of them for my film and the rejections that ought to matter, far as considerations go. However, AFRIFF is one of Africa’s largest and most competitive festivals, which makes it a reach, not a core circuit, especially for a film whose plot was not written to satisfy the mass-market taste.   

So, did ten rejections add up to a diagnosis, or is it the most informative signal there is about the film’s quality? I might as well be reading tea leaves made of silence. 

While 10 may seem like a large number, the reality check narrowed it down to 2, which then means that although it is not too early to start collecting evidence, it is certainly too hasty for me to name a cause for my rejections.  

If you're a budding filmmaker like me, learn from my mistakes. Give festival run the same intentionality as the production itself. Draw up a list of core festivals that would most likely give your film a fighting chance. Then add a couple of Academy-qualifying ones, but don't kid yourself into thinking they are anything but a reach; that manages your expectations. Thereafter, plan your film around these selected festivals in terms of their guidelines and whatnot. This is supposedly the job of the rules pages, but fuck that; they bask in deception, so check past programmes instead and count foreign titles. If not convinced, email the programmers (if an address is provided) before paying a dime.
